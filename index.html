Assumptions

Casual pay is what a working fortnight brings in; the slider is how many fortnights you expect to actually work. Fixed costs come from config (rent, bills, the B set-aside). Eating-out and giving targets are yours to set — they don't affect the runway maths, they're what the weekly trackers measure against. Income and the HELP balance drive one thing only: the deposit card on Money, which needs what an extra dollar costs you rather than your average rate. Income rather than a bracket picker, because the HELP threshold sits inside a bracket — only your actual income says which side of it you're on.

Burn

Burn is money that left Up for good, net of what was paid back to you, split by what it was for. Jar-to-jar transfers don't count, except the fortnightly B set-aside. Fortnight and Month views show days with a 7-day rolling average; longer views show weeks with a 3-week half-life trend and its flux range. Rent was higher before the July move.

Deposit, after tax

The deposit sits in savings while you decide whether to buy, and savings interest is taxed as income — so the honest comparison isn't the headline rate against inflation, it's what's left after your marginal rate. Inflation is fetched each morning from the ABS (with the RBA as a fallback) and is never guessed — if it can't be had, or the last one is too old, the real-return row is held back rather than estimated. The saver rate is in config.json; your income and HELP balance are on Strategy.

Freedom progress

The freedom number is annual spend × 25 — roughly the balance at which investment returns could cover your spending indefinitely. It moves with your spending, so trimming a habit lowers the target as well as raising the savings. Super is included in progress even though you can't touch it yet, and your HELP debt is subtracted — progress is measured on what you're actually worth, not what you hold.
